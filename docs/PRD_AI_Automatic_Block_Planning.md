## 1. Executive Summary

Indian Railways' fixed infrastructure — track (Engineering), overhead traction power (Traction Distribution/TRD), and Signalling & Telecommunication (S&T) — requires periodic maintenance blocks and disconnections to stay safe and serviceable. Today, each department independently requests these blocks through the **Block Demand Management System (BDMS)**, using maintenance data scattered across the **Track Management System (TMS)**, **Signalling Maintenance & Management System (SMMS)**, and **Traction Distribution Management System (TDMS)**, while corridor/traffic availability lives separately in the **Control Office Application (COA)**.

This decentralized, manual process causes conflicting block requests, underutilized corridor windows, uncoordinated multi-department work, and ultimately reduced asset availability and train punctuality.

This PRD defines an **AI-powered Automatic Block Planning system** that ingests defect/maintenance data from TMS, SMMS, and TDMS; combines it with corridor availability from COA (train timetable + goods traffic forecast); and uses AI/ML-driven prioritization and optimization to generate coordinated, multi-department **weekly and monthly block plans** that maximize asset uptime while respecting operational and safety constraints.

---

## 2. Background & Problem Statement

- Engineering, TRD, and S&T departments each plan maintenance blocks **independently** via BDMS.
- Maintenance need (defects, overdue tasks, condition data) is siloed in **TMS** (track), **SMMS** (signalling), and **TDMS** (traction distribution).
- Corridor/section availability — driven by the **Train Time Table** and **goods train forecast** — is managed in **COA**, disconnected from the maintenance-need systems.
- Consequences of the current manual process:
  - Conflicting or duplicate block requests on the same corridor/section
  - Poor sequencing — urgent defects wait while lower-priority work gets a slot
  - Under-utilized or fragmented block windows (short blocks that can't fit real work)
  - No cross-department coordination (e.g., Engineering and S&T could share one block but book separately)
  - Reduced asset availability, higher risk exposure, and knock-on impact to train punctuality

## 3. Goals & Objectives

| Goal | Description |
|---|---|
| **G1 — Unified data view** | Integrate defect/maintenance data (TMS, SMMS, TDMS) with corridor block availability (COA, timetable, goods forecast) into a single planning data model |
| **G2 — Intelligent prioritization** | Use AI/ML to score and rank maintenance tasks by criticality, urgency, safety risk, and asset-availability impact |
| **G3 — Optimized scheduling** | Automatically generate block schedules that minimize total asset downtime and maximize coordinated (multi-department, shared) block usage |
| **G4 — Multi-horizon planning** | Produce both a rolling **weekly** (tactical) plan and a **monthly** (strategic) plan |
| **G5 — Human-in-the-loop control** | Give planners/section controllers visibility, override, and approval capability — this is a decision-support system, not a fully autonomous one |

### Success Metrics (KPIs)

- **% increase in asset availability** (reduction in unplanned/emergency blocks caused by deferred maintenance)
- **% reduction in block-window idle time** (unused or under-utilized granted blocks)
- **% increase in multi-department co-scheduled blocks** (shared corridor blocks vs. single-department)
- **Reduction in overdue-maintenance backlog** (count and average age of overdue tasks)
- **Reduction in average time from defect report to scheduled block**
- **Planner adoption rate** (% of blocks generated by AI accepted without manual override)
- **Zero degradation in safety compliance** (all statutory/mandatory maintenance blocks still met on time)

## 4. Scope

### 4.1 In Scope
1. Data integration layer connecting TMS, SMMS, TDMS, and COA (via APIs/exports/DB replication as available)
2. AI/ML-based prioritization engine for maintenance/defect tasks
3. Optimization engine for block scheduling across corridors and departments
4. Weekly and monthly block plan generation
5. Planner-facing dashboard for review, override, and approval
6. Conflict detection and resolution suggestions across departments
7. Reporting/analytics on asset availability and block utilization

### 4.2 Out of Scope (for initial version / hackathon prototype)
- Real-time train control or signalling operations
- Direct write-back/automated execution into BDMS or COA (initial version outputs a recommended plan; execution remains human-approved)
- Rolling stock/train crew scheduling
- Full national-scale deployment (prototype targets a representative division/zone with sample or synthetic data)

| **Engineering (P.Way) Maintenance Planner** | Submit/track defect and overdue-task data, get prioritized blocks |

## 6. Functional Requirements

### FR1 — Data Integration
- FR1.1: Ingest defect and overdue-maintenance records from TMS, SMMS, TDMS (batch and/or near-real-time)
- FR1.2: Ingest corridor/section block-availability windows from COA
- FR1.3: Ingest the train timetable and goods-train traffic forecast
- FR1.4: Normalize all inputs into a common schema (asset ID, section/corridor, department, task type, defect severity, due date, estimated block duration required, dependencies)
- FR1.5: Handle missing/inconsistent data gracefully (data-quality flags, fallback defaults)

### FR2 — Task Prioritization Engine
- FR2.1: Score each maintenance/defect task using a criticality model considering: safety severity, overdue duration, asset criticality (e.g., high-density corridor vs. branch line), historical failure risk, and regulatory/statutory deadlines
- FR2.2: Support configurable weighting of prioritization factors (tunable by railway domain experts)
- FR2.3: Re-rank dynamically as new defects are reported or deadlines approach

### FR3 — Block Scheduling Optimization
- FR3.1: Given ranked tasks and available corridor windows, generate a schedule that maximizes number/criticality of tasks completed while minimizing total block time and train-service disruption
- FR3.2: Identify opportunities to **combine multiple departments' work** into a single shared block on the same corridor/time window
- FR3.3: Respect hard constraints: minimum safe block duration, mandatory possession/isolation procedures, statutory maintenance intervals, traffic-critical windows (peak goods/passenger paths) that cannot be blocked
- FR3.4: Flag and surface unresolved conflicts (e.g., two departments needing the same corridor, insufficient available windows for all urgent tasks) for human decision

### FR4 — Multi-Horizon Plan Generation
- FR4.1: Generate a **weekly** block plan (tactical, near-term, higher granularity — day/shift-level)
- FR4.2: Generate a **monthly** block plan (strategic, forward-looking, corridor/week-level)
- FR4.3: Allow the weekly plan to be regenerated/adjusted as the month progresses without breaking the monthly plan's overall targets

### FR5 — Planner Interface
- FR5.1: Dashboard showing proposed block plan (calendar/corridor timeline view)
- FR5.2: Ability to accept, reject, or manually override individual block assignments
- FR5.3: Conflict and rationale display ("why was this task prioritized/deferred")
- FR5.4: Export/reporting for weekly and monthly plans

### FR6 — Analytics & Reporting
- FR6.1: Asset availability and downtime trend reports
- FR6.2: Block utilization efficiency reports
- FR6.3: Overdue-maintenance backlog tracking

## 7. Non-Functional Requirements

| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Safety** | System is decision-support only; no automatic execution of blocks without human approval in v1 |
| **Reliability** | Scheduling engine must produce a valid (constraint-satisfying) plan even with incomplete data |
| **Explainability** | Every AI-generated priority/schedule decision must be traceable to the factors that drove it |
| **Performance** | Weekly plan generation in near-real-time (minutes); monthly plan generation within an acceptable batch window |
| **Scalability** | Architecture should scale from a single division (prototype) to zonal/national scope |
| **Security** | Role-based access aligned to department/division; audit trail of overrides and approvals |
| **Interoperability** | Integration layer built on standard APIs/adapters so it can connect to real TMS/SMMS/TDMS/COA systems or synthetic data sources for demo purposes |

## 8. Proposed System Architecture (High Level)

1. **Data Ingestion Layer** — connectors/adapters for TMS, SMMS, TDMS, COA, timetable, and goods-forecast data (real or synthetic for prototype)
2. **Unified Data Store** — normalized maintenance-task and corridor-availability data model
3. **Prioritization Engine (ML/rules hybrid)** — criticality scoring model
4. **Optimization Engine** — constraint-based/optimization algorithm (e.g., constraint programming, mixed-integer optimization, or heuristic/metaheuristic search) to assign tasks to available block windows
5. **Planning Orchestrator** — generates weekly and monthly plans, handles re-planning triggers
6. **Planner Dashboard (Web UI)** — visualization, override, approval, reporting
7. **Audit & Analytics Layer** — logs decisions, tracks KPIs

## 9. AI/ML Approach (Proposed)

- **Prioritization**: a weighted scoring model (interpretable, e.g., gradient-boosted ranking or a transparent multi-criteria scoring formula) trained/tuned on criticality, urgency, and historical impact — favor explainability over black-box models given the safety-critical domain
- **Scheduling/Optimization**: formulate as a constrained assignment/scheduling optimization problem (tasks × available block windows, subject to duration, department, and traffic constraints); solve via optimization solvers (e.g., OR-Tools, mixed-integer programming) or metaheuristics (genetic algorithm/simulated annealing) if the search space is too large for exact solving
- **Continuous learning**: feedback loop where actual outcomes (blocks completed vs. planned, overrides made by planners) refine future prioritization weights

## 10. Assumptions & Dependencies

- Sample/representative datasets for TMS, SMMS, TDMS, and COA will be used for the prototype, since live system access is unlikely during a hackathon
- Domain rules (minimum block durations, statutory maintenance intervals, traffic-critical windows) will be gathered from publicly available Indian Railways maintenance/safety guidelines and reasonable assumptions, to be validated with domain mentors
- Final deployment would require integration approval and data-sharing agreements with the respective Indian Railways systems (TMS, SMMS, TDMS, COA/BDMS)

## 11. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Incomplete/inaccessible real railway data | Build a realistic synthetic data generator matching real system schemas |
| Over-trusting AI for safety-critical scheduling | Keep human-in-the-loop approval mandatory in v1; full explainability of every recommendation |
| Optimization becomes computationally intractable at scale | Start with heuristic/greedy baseline, then layer in exact/metaheuristic optimization; test at division scale first |
| Resistance to a new unified process from siloed departments | Design for coordination, not replacement — each department still owns its own task data and gets visibility/benefit from combined blocks |
